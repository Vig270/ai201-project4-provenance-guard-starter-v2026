## Milestone 4: Combining Detection Signals

Provenance Guard combines two heuristic signals to produce a single score.

### Scoring approach

The function `scoring.py::combine_signals` calculates a weighted average:

- **Model signal: 70%**
- **Style signal: 30%**

The combined score is calculated as:

`combined_score = 0.7 * model_score + 0.3 * style_score`

The configured thresholds are:

- **Human:** combined score at or below `0.35`
- **Unsure:** combined score above `0.35` and below `0.65`
- **AI:** combined score at or above `0.65`

The returned confidence value represents the score's distance from the midpoint of `0.5`. It is a heuristic, not a calibrated probability that the classification is correct.

### Sample evaluation

| Sample | Model score | Style score | Combined score | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Clearly AI-like | 0.8275 | 0.2899 | 0.6662 | AI |
| Clearly human | 0.5287 | 0.3974 | 0.4893 | Unsure |
| Formal human | 0.5261 | 0.2821 | 0.4529 | Unsure |
| Lightly edited AI | 0.3137 | 0.3951 | 0.3381 | Human |

### Limitations

These examples demonstrate that the scoring pipeline works, but they do not establish detector accuracy. The clearly AI-like sample was classified as AI, while the clearly human sample remained inconclusive and the lightly edited AI sample was classified as human.

Writing style and model scores can overlap between human-written and AI-generated text. The results should therefore be treated as heuristic signals, not proof of authorship. More representative labeled data and systematic evaluation would be needed to assess accuracy and tune the thresholds responsibly.


## Label Variants

Provenance Guard uses three reader-facing labels based on the combined score. These labels communicate a tentative assessment, not proof of authorship.

| Category | Score range | Exact label shown to the writer |
|---|---|---|
| Human | 0.00–0.35, inclusive | We think this text is more consistent with human writing, but we can't be certain who wrote it. |
| Unsure | Above 0.35 and below 0.65 | We couldn't determine whether this text was written by a person or generated by AI. This result isn't a judgment about your work. |
| AI | 0.65–1.00, inclusive | We think this text may have been generated by AI, but this result isn't proof of who wrote it. |

The `human` category includes scores at or below 0.35. The `ai` category includes scores at or above 0.65. Scores between those boundaries receive the `unsure` category.

## Rate Limiting

**My limits:** 5 per minute, 50 per day

**Why those numbers and not others:**
I chose five submissions per minute to allow legitimate writers to submit their work while restricting scripts that rapidly send variations to discover the detector's thresholds. The daily limit of 50 provides additional protection against repeated automated submissions.

**What a caller counts as:** A caller is identified by their creator_id in the JSON request. If a valid creator_id is not provided, the system falls back to the request's IP address. ### 4. Five-Stage Failure Analysis

The system's decision pipeline consists of five stages:

1. **Route:** `app.py::submit` receives and validates the request.
2. **Signal One:** `detector.py::model_signal` estimates AI-likeness using GPT-2 perplexity.
3. **Signal Two:** `stylometry.py::style_signal` evaluates writing characteristics such as sentence-length variation, word diversity, and punctuation density.
4. **Combining Rule:** `scoring.py::combine_signals` combines the signals using 70% model signal and 30% style signal.
5. **Label Mapping:** `scoring.py::score_to_label` maps the combined score to `human`, `unsure`, or `ai`.

### 5. Patterns and Overall Findings

The evaluation and attack results revealed several patterns:

1. **The model signal is a major source of classification errors.** It produced high scores for several human-written samples and low scores for altered AI-generated samples.
2. **The style signal did not consistently compensate for model-signal errors.** The 70/30 weighting favored the model signal, which contributed to the five false positives.
3. **The middle confidence range was not adequately represented in the label-coverage evaluation.** All three samples received `unsure`.
4. **Input validation needs stronger coverage.** Control-character-only and punctuation-only text, along with invalid creator identifiers, were accepted.
5. **Rate limiting worked in the tested flood scenario.** Seven submissions beyond the initial five were blocked.
6. **The appeal workflow showed successful individual transitions, but the required five attempts were not demonstrated.**
7. **Evaluation coverage was incomplete.** The score-spread scenario was missing, and the label-coverage scenario was assigned the wrong criterion number.

### 6. Conclusion

Milestone 4 showed that the system successfully handled several basic protections, including the tested self-written samples, common malformed requests, and rapid repeated submissions. However, the broader attack tests exposed weaknesses in AI-text classification, false-positive prevention, input validation, and evaluation coverage.

The most significant classification issue was the model signal's tendency to assign high scores to certain human-written samples while assigning low scores to some altered AI-generated samples. The 70% model weighting allowed these signal outputs to strongly influence the final classification.

The label-mapping and combining functions generally behaved according to their configured rules. The results therefore point primarily to signal quality and test coverage as areas requiring further investigation, rather than evidence that the threshold logic itself malfunctioned.

The original success criteria and thresholds were preserved. Unmeasured or incompletely tested criteria were documented honestly instead of being lowered to produce a passing result.

## Milestone 5: Fix One Thing and Re-run

### 1. Improvement selected

Based on the Milestone 4 diagnosis, I decided to adjust the weights assigned to the two scoring signals. The original configuration gave the model signal too much influence over the combined score, contributing to false-positive AI classifications in human-written samples.

The original weights were:

- Model signal: 70%
- Style signal: 30%

I changed the weights to:

- Model signal: 50%
- Style signal: 50%

This was the only scoring change made for this experiment. I kept both label thresholds unchanged:

- Human threshold: 0.35
- AI threshold: 0.65

**Reason for the change:** Reducing the model signal's influence and giving the style signal equal weight could help prevent human-written text from receiving high-confidence AI labels.

### 2. Before-and-after attack results

I ran the same 56 attacks using `python run_attacks.py --set attack_sets --label before` and `python run_attacks.py --set attack_sets --label after`.

|---|---|---|
| Evasion | 3 human, 6 unsure, 1 AI | 4 human, 5 unsure, 1 AI |
| False positives | 5 AI, 2 unsure, 1 human | 0 AI, 7 unsure, 1 human |
| Prompt injection | 1 human, 5 unsure | 2 human, 4 unsure |
| Malformed inputs | 4 accepted with HTTP 200; remaining requests rejected or rate-limited | Same results |
| Flood protection | 5 accepted, 7 blocked with HTTP 429 | 5 accepted, 7 blocked with HTTP 429 |

**Important observations:**

- False-positive AI classifications decreased from five to zero. FP03–FP07 changed from `ai` to `unsure`.
- Evasion results changed slightly: EV06 changed from `unsure` to `human`.
- Prompt injection results changed: PI01 changed from `unsure` to `human`, while PI02 remained `human`.
- Malformed-input handling did not improve. MF03, MF04, RQ02, and RQ06 continued to receive HTTP 200.
- Rate limiting continued to work, blocking all seven requests beyond the five-request allowance.

### 3. Before-and-after evaluation results

I also ran `python run_eval.py --label before` and `python run_eval.py --label after`, with three trials for each scenario currently configured in `scenarios.py`.

### 4. Did the improvement help?

Yes, the change helped reduce false-positive AI classifications in the attack set. The number of human-written attack samples incorrectly labeled as AI decreased from five to zero.

However, the improvement introduced trade-offs. Some evasion and prompt-injection samples received human labels instead of unsure labels. This shows that reducing the model signal's influence can reduce false positives but may also make certain AI-generated or adversarial samples harder to distinguish.

The label coverage evaluation also remained unchanged, with all three samples receiving the `unsure` label. The experiment therefore did not solve the label coverage issue.

Overall, the 50/50 weighting improved one important area without fixing every weakness. The results demonstrate why changes to a detection system should be measured against multiple types of tests rather than judged by one metric alone.
